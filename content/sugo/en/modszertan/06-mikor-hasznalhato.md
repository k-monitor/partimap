# When can online surveys and PARTIMAP be used?

Online surveys are a fast, cost-effective and simple way to collect opinions. With PARTIMAP, you can reach a large number of respondents in a short time, and collect map-based feedback alongside traditional survey responses.

The platform is especially recommended for projects with a spatial dimension, such as:

- municipal and urban development;
- identifying transport problems;
- developing parks, public squares or green spaces;
- evaluating cycling and walking routes;
- examining the location of community services;
- post-project evaluation of investments.

With PARTIMAP, participants can not only answer questions but also mark points, lines or areas on the map. This makes it possible to display problems, development proposals, favorite places or points of conflict spatially.

The advantage of online surveys is their low barrier to entry: many people take part in surveys that they would not join at an in-person forum or public event. This is especially useful for collecting opinions widely and mapping community needs.

However, it is important to consider the limitations of the method as well. Online surveys are less effective at reaching target groups who rarely use the internet or lack suitable digital devices. In addition, respondents take part in the survey voluntarily, so the results cannot always be considered representative.

PARTIMAP is primarily suited to uncovering problems, gathering ideas, evaluating development alternatives and summarizing map-based feedback. In complex or contentious issues where dialogue and joint thinking among participants also matter, it is worth complementing surveys with in-person participatory methods – such as workshops, public forums, interviews or focus groups.

The most effective participatory processes usually combine several engagement methods. Among these, PARTIMAP is a flexible, easy-to-use tool that helps gather community knowledge and lay the groundwork for decisions.
