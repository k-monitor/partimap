# What other tools can be used for engagement?

Successful participatory processes are usually not built on a single tool. The best results are achieved when different engagement methods are applied so that they build on one another. PARTIMAP combines well with other online and in-person participation techniques.

## Informing the public

The first step of engagement is making sure that stakeholders hear about the project at all. For this, you can use:

- social media;
- newsletters;
- websites;
- posters and flyers;
- local newspapers and radio stations;
- local community groups and mailing lists.

## Online consultation

Online surveys offer a fast and cost-effective way to collect opinions. PARTIMAP is especially useful when the feedback relates to specific locations and respondents want to mark their suggestions or problems on a map.

## In-person participation methods

For more complex issues or those attracting greater public interest, it is worth organizing in-person meetings as well, such as:

- public forums;
- workshops;
- focus group discussions;
- community planning sessions;
- professional working groups.

These give participants the opportunity not only to express their opinions but also to discuss different perspectives with one another.

## Combining methods

PARTIMAP fits well into a longer participatory process. For example, a project can start with a map-based survey that identifies problems and gathers residents' suggestions. Based on the results, workshops or public forums can be organized where participants work out possible solutions together. At the end of the process, another survey can be used to evaluate the alternatives developed or the improvements implemented.

## Broad and deep engagement

Engagement methods require different levels of participation.

- **Broad (extensive) engagement** allows many people to give their opinion quickly and easily, for example through online surveys or polls.
- **Deep (intensive) engagement** involves smaller groups taking part in face-to-face discussions, where different perspectives can be discussed in detail and joint solutions developed.

In general, the more complex or contentious an issue, the greater the need for in-person participation methods. In these processes, PARTIMAP mainly supports collecting and organizing opinions and preparing decisions.
