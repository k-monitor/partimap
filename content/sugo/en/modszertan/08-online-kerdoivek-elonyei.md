# Advantages and limitations of online surveys

Online surveys are a fast and cost-effective tool for collecting opinions. Respondents can answer at any time, even from home, which makes it easier to reach people who would not attend a forum or workshop in person.

## Advantages of PARTIMAP

- Surveys are easy to create and share.
- Map-based and traditional questions can be combined.
- The survey can also be password-protected, so that only invited people can fill it in.
- Images, maps and other visual elements can help respondents answer.
- Especially suitable for mapping local problems, development ideas and patterns of space use.

## What should you watch out for?

- Online surveys do not reach all target groups equally, so it is worth complementing them with other engagement methods.
- Instead of overly general or theoretical questions, ask about specific situations and decisions.
- An exploratory survey is a good starting point, but on its own it is rarely enough to carry out a complex participatory process.

## Community survey or representative research?

PARTIMAP is primarily designed for creating **community surveys**. These let you quickly learn about a community's opinions, problems and suggestions, even if the results are not statistically representative of the entire population.

If you need accurate, representative data about a larger population, you need a professional opinion poll. The two methods can complement each other well: PARTIMAP can help uncover the main problems and patterns, which can later be explored further through focus groups, interviews or representative research.

### Engagement based on in-person participation

In-person engagement requires more time and organization than an online survey, but in return it allows for genuine dialogue and joint thinking. It is especially useful in complex or controversial issues where it is important to understand the arguments and to weigh different viewpoints against each other.

#### When should you choose in-person methods?

- If the topic strongly affects the local community.
- If the decision affects several interest groups.
- If you want to develop not only opinions but also joint solutions.

### Common in-person engagement methods

#### Focus groups

- Small-group discussions that help you understand the participants' opinions, motivations and arguments in more depth.

#### Public forums

- Public events where anyone can share their opinion. Proper moderation and the presence of decision-makers are important for success.

#### On-site pop-up stands

- With short conversations and surveys in busy public spaces or at the development site, you can also reach people who would not get involved online.

#### Community events

- Walks, site visits, workshops, brainstorming sessions or other community events can help participants share their experiences directly on site.

### Online and in-person engagement together

The best results usually come from combining the two methods. With PARTIMAP, you can quickly reach many people and gather the most important problems, then build on these to discuss possible solutions in more detail at in-person forums or workshops.

> **Tip:** An online survey helps uncover the main opinions, while in-person meetings provide an opportunity to interpret and develop them together. Together, the two methods support genuine participation and better decision-making.
