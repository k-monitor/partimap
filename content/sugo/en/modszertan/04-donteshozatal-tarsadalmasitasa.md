# Involving the public in decision-making

Involving the public means that stakeholders' views do not merely appear as a formality in a decision-making process, but play a real role in preparing it. The aim of the participatory process is for decision-makers to take into account the experiences, needs and suggestions of the local community alongside professional considerations.

The key to success is for the survey to be part of a well-designed decision-making process whose goals, framework and expected outcomes are clear to all participants.

Before designing the survey, it is worth clarifying the following questions:

- What is the purpose of the decision, and on which issues is genuine public feedback expected?
- At which stage of the process does engagement take place: problem identification, planning, implementation or evaluation?
- Who are the most important stakeholders in the decision, and how can they be reached?
- How will the responses received be processed and evaluated?
- When and in what form will participants receive feedback on the results?

A participatory process is credible if participants know exactly what their answers will be used for. It is worth presenting the purpose of the project, the framework for decision-making, and which issues are genuinely open to change right at the beginning of the survey. If certain elements have already been decided, you should make this clear as well.

Involving the public does not end with data collection. To maintain trust, it is important for the project owner to share the survey results, present the key conclusions, and inform participants how the opinions received influenced the final decisions. This also significantly increases the success of future participatory processes.
