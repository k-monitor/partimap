# Recommended questionnaire structure

A well-structured questionnaire is clear, easy to complete, and helps you collect reliable data. A questionnaire contains mandatory and optional elements.

### Mandatory elements:

- welcome page and brief information,
- privacy notice and consent statement (if necessary),
- the main block of questions,
- thank-you message.

### Optional elements:

- title page (mainly for printed questionnaires),
- instructions for completion,
- screening questions,
- warm-up questions,
- sensitive or demographic questions,
- request for feedback on the questionnaire.

The title of the questionnaire should be short, easy to understand and informative. It should avoid jargon, overly long wording, and phrasing that could influence respondents or that only draws attention to a prize.

The welcome page should briefly describe the purpose of the questionnaire, the organizer, the expected time needed to complete it, and – where necessary – information on data processing and the conditions of participation. If the research requires it, the consent statement should also appear here.

It is advisable to start the questionnaire with simple warm-up questions, followed by the questions most important for the research. Sensitive and demographic questions are generally best placed at the end of the questionnaire, so that they don't reduce people's willingness to complete it.

The structure of a typical questionnaire:

## 1. Introduction

Briefly explain:

- who created the questionnaire,
- what its purpose is,
- what the results will be used for,
- how long it takes to complete.

Keep the introduction short. A few well-worded sentences are enough.

## 2. Privacy notice

If you collect personal data, explain:

- what data you process,
- for what purpose,
- how long you store it,
- how to contact the data controller.

If the questionnaire is anonymous, it is worth stating this clearly as well.

## 3. Warm-up questions

The first questions should be easy to answer.

For example, in a questionnaire about an urban development:

- How often do you come to this area?
- Which mode of transport do you use most often?

These help get the respondent engaged.

## 4. The main block of questions

This is where the questions that actually address the research objectives belong.

The most important questions of the questionnaire, those serving the research objectives, should ideally appear after the first third of the questionnaire. By then, respondents have settled into the questionnaire, but their attention has not yet started to wane.

Arrange the questions in a logical order: move from more general topics to more specific ones (following the so-called *funnel principle*). It is best to keep questions on the same topic together in one block and to avoid switching between topics without good reason.

If a topic includes both open and closed questions, it is generally recommended to ask the open questions first, so that the respondent's opinion is not influenced by the predefined answer options.

It is worth grouping the questions by topic.

**For example:**

- transport,
- parking,
- public spaces,
- green spaces,
- public safety.

Don't keep switching between topics.

## 5. Sensitive questions

### Sensitive questions

Sensitive questions concern topics that respondents may consider personal or confidential, such as health, income, political views, sexual orientation, or alcohol and drug use.

It is best to place these questions at the end of the questionnaire. By then, respondents are already committed to completing it, so they are more likely to answer these as well. If they do decide to stop, the answers they gave earlier can still be used in most online surveys.

## 6. Demographic questions

Sociodemographic questions, such as those about age, gender, level of education, income or household composition, are sometimes placed at the beginning of the questionnaire, but in many cases at the end, unless they serve as screening questions.

Only ask for the demographic data you really need to analyze the results. Unnecessary questions increase completion time and can reduce people's willingness to respond.

## 7. Thank-you message

Always thank respondents for completing the questionnaire.

If you like, you can provide further information here, such as:

- where the results will be available,
- when the project is expected to continue,
- whom respondents can contact with questions.
