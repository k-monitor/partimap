# Choosing the right question type

Choosing the right question type affects the quality of the answers and also how easily the results can be analyzed. PARTIMAP supports several question types, so it is worth always choosing the one that best fits the purpose of the question.

## Closed questions

With closed questions, the respondent chooses from predefined answer options. They are quick to complete, easy to evaluate, and produce easily comparable results. However, they can only capture the predefined answers, so they are less suitable for uncovering new or unexpected opinions.

The most common closed question types:

- Single choice – the respondent can select one answer option.
- Multiple selection – several answers can be selected at once.
- Two-option (yes/no) question – can be used for simple decisions.
- Rating scale – used to measure opinion, satisfaction or importance.

## Open questions

With an open question, respondents can phrase their answer in their own words. This is especially useful if you want to collect detailed opinions, suggestions, or information for which not every possible answer option can be specified in advance.

Answering open questions takes more time, and processing the results is also more complex. For this reason, it is best to use them only when justified, and where possible, they should make up only a small share of the questions in the questionnaire.

## “Other” answer option

If you are not sure that the predefined answers cover every possible case, it is worth providing an **“Other”** answer option as well.

The “Other” option can be:

- **without a text field**, if it is enough to know that the answer does not fit the listed categories;
- **with a text field**, if it is important to learn the respondent's own answer.

## Rating scales

Rating scales can be used to measure, for example, the level of satisfaction, importance or agreement. The most commonly used scales have 5 or 7 answer options, as these strike a good balance between detail and ease of completion.

It is best to use the same scale consistently throughout the questionnaire, as this makes it easier for respondents to interpret the questions.

## Likert scale

The Likert scale is one of the most widely used rating methods. It allows respondents to express their agreement with a statement, or their rating of a given attribute, on a multi-point scale.

**Example:**

- Strongly disagree
- Somewhat disagree
- Neutral
- Somewhat agree
- Strongly agree

In PARTIMAP surveys, Likert-type scales are particularly well suited to satisfaction and opinion surveys.

## Good practices

When choosing a question type, it is worth following a few simple rules:

- use a closed question if the possible answers can be defined in advance;
- use an open question if you want to collect detailed opinions or suggestions;
- only use long text answer fields when justified;
- provide an “Other” answer option if the listed categories do not fully cover the possible answers;
- where possible, use consistent rating scales throughout the questionnaire, so that respondents find it easier to answer.
