# What makes a good question?

A question should be:

- short,
- unambiguous,
- easy to understand,
- about a single topic.

Avoid long sentences.

### Bad example

How satisfied are you with the cleanliness, lighting and safety of the park?

This contains three different questions.

### Good example

How satisfied are you with the cleanliness of the park?

## Wording the questions

The wording of the questions fundamentally determines the quality of the answers. An imprecise, ambiguous or leading question can bias the results, so when creating a questionnaire, pay particular attention to clear and unambiguous wording.

When writing questions, it is worth following these basic principles:

- Use simple, easy-to-understand language.
- Avoid jargon, foreign words and complicated sentence structures.
- Questions should be short, unambiguous and about one topic only.
- Aim for neutral wording that does not suggest the “right” answer.
- The form of address should be consistent and suited to the target group; for general public surveys, it is advisable to use the formal form of address.

Before finalizing the questionnaire, it is worth reviewing each question individually to see whether it contains any wording errors that could influence the answers. Pay particular attention to ambiguous questions, questions that combine several questions in one (double-barreled questions) and leading questions, and rephrase them if necessary before the test run.
