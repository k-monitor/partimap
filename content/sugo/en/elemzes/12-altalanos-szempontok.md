# General considerations for data analysis

## Summary

When analyzing data, it is worth keeping a few basic principles in mind. They help make your analysis well thought out, consistent and reliable.

## 1. Define the purpose of the analysis

Before you start the analysis, think about which questions you want answered. You don't need to analyze every variable in the dataset; it is enough to select those needed to answer the question at hand.

For example, if you want to know what men and women think about a community space, it is enough to examine the gender and satisfaction variables.

## 2. Consider the level of measurement of the variables

Before every analysis, consider the level of measurement of the variable in question, because it determines which statistical operations you can perform on it.

For example, with a five-point satisfaction scale, you can calculate:

- the average rating of the total sample,
- the average score of men and of women,
- or what share of the different groups gave, for example, a rating of 5.

## 3. Look for relationships

Don't just examine each question separately; think about how they relate to one another.

For example, if the survey asked respondents how they spend their free time and also how often they do sports, analyzing the two questions together can lead to interesting conclusions. It may turn out, for example, that more older people do sports, while younger people do sports more frequently.

## 4. Interpret the results

Presenting data is not, in itself, analysis. Try to find explanations for the differences you observe.

For example, if you see that women are less satisfied with a community space than men, it is worth examining in which specific areas their opinions differ most. If women rate public safety lower, this may also explain their lower overall satisfaction.

## 5. Check the results

If a result seems surprising or unusual, always check:

- whether you analyzed the right variables,
- whether the calculations are correct,
- whether the numbers of cases are adequate,
- and whether there was a data handling error.

Checking helps you avoid drawing the wrong conclusions.

## 6. Consider the limitations of the analysis

Interpret every result in light of who filled in the survey and who might have been left out of it.

Think about:

- whose views the results reflect,
- which groups you managed to reach,
- and which groups may be underrepresented.

Only draw conclusions with these in mind, and communicate clearly to whom the results apply.
