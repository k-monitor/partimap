# Basic demographic variables

Before you start analyzing the substantive questions of the survey, it is worth examining the composition of your sample, that is, the demographic characteristics of the respondents.

## Representative sample

If you are working with a representative sample, the proportions of the different demographic groups should reflect the actual distribution of the entire population under study. Any deviation from this is usually corrected by weighting.

Representativeness means that the distributions measured in the sample match – or at least come close to – the distributions of the population along characteristics such as:

- gender,
- age,
- level of education,
- type of settlement,
- region (in national studies).

Another important condition is that the sample is selected at random, meaning that every member of the population has an equal chance of being included in the sample.

If these conditions are met, the results obtained from the sample can be generalized to the entire population; for example, you can draw conclusions about the whole population of a municipality.

## If the sample is not representative

Even if you are not working with a representative sample, or you don't know the characteristics of the whole population, it is still worth reviewing who filled in the survey. This helps you interpret the results correctly and understand the limits of your conclusions.

Non-representative data collection does not mean that the results are worthless. The aim of many studies is not to represent the views of an entire municipality, organization or target group in a statistical sense, but to learn more about the experiences, opinions or needs of those who completed the survey.

Such surveys can also provide a wealth of useful information. For example, they can help you:

- uncover the most important problems or areas for improvement,
- identify the respondents' needs and opinions,
- compare the answers of different groups,
- lay the groundwork for development or strategic decisions,
- formulate new research questions.

The most important thing is that when communicating the results, you always make it clear who took part in the survey and that the findings apply to those who completed it. If you keep this in mind, even a non-representative survey can provide valuable and highly usable information.

## Which demographic data should you examine?

The most frequently analyzed demographic variables are:

- gender,
- age,
- place of residence,
- level of education.

Depending on the purpose of the research, you can supplement these with further variables, such as:

- marital status,
- employment status.
