# Step 3: Designing the questionnaire

## Putting together the questions

Start creating the questionnaire by gathering the topics you want answers on.

Then:

1. organize them into thematic blocks;
2. decide the order of the blocks;
3. write the individual questions;
4. finally, simplify and refine the wording.

This gives you a much clearer questionnaire that is easier to complete.

## How long should the questionnaire be?

Experience shows that it's best to create a questionnaire that:

- can be completed in 10–15 minutes at most;
- ideally has no more than 30 questions.

Many people abandon questionnaires that are too long, so shorter, focused questionnaires usually achieve better completion rates.

To estimate the completion time, you can start from this simple rule:

- answering a closed question takes about 20 seconds on average;
- answering an open question takes 40 seconds on average.

Based on this, you can easily estimate the total time needed to complete the questionnaire.

## Question types

It's worth using several question types, as this makes the questionnaire more varied and reduces respondent fatigue.

### Closed questions

Closed questions offer predefined answer options, so they are easy to answer and simple to analyze.

**Examples:**

- “How satisfied are you with local public spaces?” (scale from 1 to 5)
- “Are you satisfied with local transport?” (Yes/No)
- “How often do you use local bus services?” (Daily, weekly, monthly, never)

### Open questions

Open questions let respondents express their views in their own words.

**Example:**

- “How would you improve the local transport network?”

Although they can produce more valuable, detailed answers, analyzing them takes much more time.

## The order of the questions

When structuring the questionnaire, move from simpler questions to more complex ones.

### Introduction

At the beginning of the questionnaire, it's advisable to:

- briefly present the purpose of the survey;
- provide information on data processing (GDPR);
- explain what the answers will be used for.

The first questions should be simple, so that respondents can easily get started with the questionnaire.

This section often includes demographic questions, such as:

- gender;
- age;
- place of residence;
- level of education.

### Main part

This is where the questions on the central topic of the questionnaire belong.

Ask more general questions first, then gradually move on to more detailed ones.

**For example:**

- “How often do you use local bus services?”
- “Why do you choose the bus over other modes of transport?”

### Closing part

At the end of the questionnaire, thank respondents for taking part and give them the chance to share any further comments.

**For example:**

“Is there anything else you would like to share with us about this topic?”

## Wording the questions

Well-worded questions fundamentally determine the quality of the answers. It's worth following a few simple rules:

- write clearly and in plain language;
- avoid long, complicated sentences and jargon;
- keep the questions short and to the point;
- for closed questions, make sure the answer options cover all possible answers;
- each question should always address only one topic.

**Bad example:**

Are you satisfied with the local buses and trains?

This question asks about two different topics at once.

**Good solution:**

- Are you satisfied with local bus services?
- Are you satisfied with rail services?
