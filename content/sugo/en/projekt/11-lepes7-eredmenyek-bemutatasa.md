# Step 7: Presenting the results and advocacy

A PARTIMAP project truly achieves its goal when the collected data lead to genuine dialogue and, ideally, to real change as well.

Once the analysis is done, think about whom you want to present the results to.

Possible target groups:

- local residents;
- the local government;
- decision-makers;
- civil society organizations;
- experts;
- the media;
- the respondents of the questionnaire.

## Present the results in an accessible way

Not everyone understands statistics, so it's worth:

- using simple charts;
- presenting the key results on maps;
- highlighting the most important findings;
- preparing short summaries.

Less is often more: a few easy-to-read charts say more than many pages of tables.

## Link the data to proposals

Data alone rarely lead to change.

For every important result, it's worth formulating a possible solution.

**For example:**

- if many people complain about the lack of pedestrian crossings, you can propose new crosswalks;
- if a park is used by many people but has few benches, you can put forward an improvement proposal;
- if cyclists consider a route dangerous, it's worth pushing for infrastructure improvements.

## Involve the community in what comes next

Advocacy is much more successful if the community not only fills in the questionnaire but also takes part in discussing the results.

This can happen, for example:

- at public forums;
- at workshops;
- at public presentations;
- in online consultations;
- in the form of petitions or community campaigns.

## Close the project with feedback

Respondents took the time to fill in the questionnaire, so it's important that they get feedback on what happened with the results.

Share with them:

- how many responses came in;
- what the main results were;
- what decisions or developments have been set in motion;
- what next steps can be expected.

This builds trust, increases the community's engagement, and significantly improves the chances that people will take an active part in future surveys too.
