# Step 5: Distributing the questionnaire

A well-designed questionnaire will only be successful if it reaches the people whose opinions you want to hear. That's why distribution deserves at least as much attention as putting the questionnaire together.

The most effective results usually come from combining online and offline tools.

## Online channels

For most local projects, these are the fastest and cheapest option.

You can use, for example:

- local Facebook groups;
- your own Facebook, Instagram or LinkedIn page;
- newsletters;
- the mailing lists of civil society organizations;
- local online newspapers;
- municipal websites.

## Offline distribution

Not every target group can be reached online, so it's worth using in-person channels as well.

**For example:**

- the local government newspaper;
- posters and flyers;
- libraries;
- community cultural centers;
- schools;
- markets;
- public forums;
- mailbox drops.

It's a good idea to put a QR code on printed materials that leads directly to the questionnaire.

## Partnerships

Local partners can be a great help in getting the questionnaire to people you wouldn't reach through your own channels.

It's worth working with:

- local businesses;
- cafés;
- bakeries;
- cultural institutions;
- local media;
- the local government;
- civil society organizations;
- sports clubs;
- local opinion leaders.

## Think about whom you haven't reached

During distribution, it's always worth checking whether there are groups that are likely to be left out.

**For example:**

- older age groups;
- people who rarely use the internet;
- people with lower levels of education;
- residents of smaller neighborhoods.

If necessary, you can also use separate data collection methods for them (for example, a paper questionnaire or going out to meet people in person).
