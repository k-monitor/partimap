# Step 6: Analyzing the incoming data

Data collection is only one half of the project. The real value comes when you can draw clear, usable conclusions from the answers.

The first step of the analysis is always data cleaning.

## Check the data

Before you start calculating, review the dataset.

For example, it's worth filtering out:

- incomplete responses;
- duplicate answers;
- obvious errors;
- unrealistic or automated submissions;
- respondents who selected the same value for every question.

## Get to know the sample

First, always look at:

- how many respondents you had;
- their gender distribution;
- which age groups responded;
- where the respondents came from;
- how well the sample reflects the community you want to talk about.

## Look for connections

Beyond simple frequencies, it's worth examining:

- which problems came up most often;
- in which parts of the city certain phenomena occur;
- whether the answers of different demographic groups differ from each other;
- what patterns emerge on the map.

## Compare with other data

If earlier surveys or official statistics are available, it's worth comparing your results with them.

This helps you decide whether:

- it is a new problem;
- you are seeing a lasting trend;
- or you have measured a one-off phenomenon.

## Mind the limits of your conclusions

Most PARTIMAP surveys are not representative.

So it's better to phrase your findings like this:

- “According to the respondents of the questionnaire…”
- “Most respondents believe…”

and not like this:

- “According to the residents of the city…”

This wording is more accurate and professionally sound.

## What software can you use?

Several free or widely used programs are suitable for the analysis:

- **Microsoft Excel** – for basic statistics, tables and charts;
- **QGIS** – for spatial analysis;
- **Google My Maps** – for simple map visualization;
- **Flourish or Tableau** – for creating interactive visualizations.
