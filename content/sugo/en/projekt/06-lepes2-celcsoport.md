# Step 2: Defining the target group

Successful community data collection depends not only on a well-designed questionnaire, but also on **who the survey reaches** and whom you involve in the different stages of the project.

Right at the start of planning, think through who the stakeholders are who may play an important role in data collection, in interpreting the results or in later implementation.

## Local residents

For most PARTIMAP projects, the primary target group is local residents. Their experiences and opinions form the basis of the survey.

You can get the questionnaire to them through several channels, for example:

- on social media;
- in local newsletters;
- at local community or civil society events;
- through the local press.

## Experts

Depending on the topic of the project, it's worth involving experts in interpreting the results as well. Their experience can help interpret the data correctly and can increase the credibility of the research.

## Local government

If the survey deals with local issues, it's a good idea to get in touch with the local government right at the start of the project.

If decision-makers follow the process all the way through, they are more likely to accept the research results and use them in later planning.

## Civil society organizations and local communities

Local civil society organizations, community groups and activists can be a great help in promoting the questionnaire, reaching respondents and mobilizing residents.

Of course, every project is different. For example, a local government and a civil society organization may jointly launch a resident survey, or a business may work together with local communities. The most important thing is to think through, right at the start of the project, whom it's worth involving.
